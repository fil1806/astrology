Step 3. The Houses

The most important part of the natal chart. There are twelve houses, mirroring the twelve signs — but where the signs describe how a planet acts, the houses tell you in which area of life it plays out.

The houses are calculated from the exact time and place of birth, which is why a complete chart can't do without them. Each house echoes the meaning of its corresponding sign: the 1st house relates to Aries, the 2nd to Taurus, and so on. It's a handy anchor for memorizing them.

The Angles

  • Ascendant (As) — the cusp of the 1st house, the rising point. Appearance, behavior, the way a person "enters" the world.
  • Descendant (Ds) — the cusp of the 7th house. Partners and open adversaries.
  • Midheaven (MC, Medium Coeli) — the cusp of the 10th house. Goals, career, one's social peak.
  • Imum Coeli (IC) — the cusp of the 4th house. Roots, home, family, ancestry.

Planets on the angles express themselves especially strongly in a person's life.

How to Read a Planet in a House

The reading formula: planet (what kind of energy) + sign (how it expresses itself) + house (in which area of life). For example, Mars in Capricorn in the 10th house: the energy of action (Mars), disciplined and goal-driven (Capricorn), aimed at career and status (10th house) — a career-minded person with an enormous capacity for work.

The ruler of the house matters too: the sign on a house cusp has a ruling planet, and that planet's placement shows where the house's theme "comes from". If the ruler of the 10th house sits in the 9th, the career may be tied to foreign countries, academia or teaching.

The houses are what set a real natal chart apart from the newspaper "Sun-sign horoscope". That's exactly why calculating them requires an accurate birth time — try to find it out to within at least 10–15 minutes.
