Step 4. Aspects Between the Planets

An aspect is the angular distance between planets in a chart. Aspects show how the planets' energies interact: whether they support each other, clash, or merge into one.

There are five main (major) aspects. Harmonious aspects are traditionally drawn in blue or black, challenging ones in red — these are exactly the lines you see inside the circle of a natal chart.

The Five Major Aspects

AspectAngleNatureMeaning
☌ Conjunction0°Neutral (depends on the planets)The energies fuse into one: the planets always act together, amplifying each other. A conjunction with Venus is a gift; with Saturn, a test.
✶ Sextile60°HarmoniousOpportunity: a cooperation of energies that has to be activated through effort. "The door is open — but you have to walk through it yourself."
□ Square90°ChallengingA conflict of energies, inner tension, obstacles. Yet it's the squares that provide motivation and build resilience — the charts of strong people are full of them.
△ Trine120°HarmoniousAn easy, natural flow: a talent you're born with. The danger is laziness, since a trine works even without effort.
☍ Opposition180°ChallengingA standoff: the energies pull in opposite directions, often playing out through other people and relationships. The task is to find a balance between the two poles.

Orbs

An exact aspect is rare, so some deviation from the precise angle is allowed — this is the orb. Typically up to 7–10° is allowed for the luminaries, up to 5–7° for the planets, and 1–2° for minor aspects. The more exact the aspect, the more strongly it works.

Aspect Patterns

Several aspects can combine into figures that work as a single mechanism:

  • Grand Trine — three planets in trine to one another: a closed circuit of talent, often within a single element.
  • T-square — an opposition plus two squares to a third planet: a powerful engine of tension that demands an outlet.
  • Grand Cross — two oppositions and four squares: maximum tension and maximum toughening.
  • Bi-sextile — an opposition "defused" by two sextiles and trines: tension with ready-made paths to resolution.

How to Read an Aspect

The formula: planet A + nature of the aspect + planet B. For example:

  • Mercury △ Jupiter — a broad mind, ease of learning, a gift for teaching.
  • Mars □ Saturn — action runs up against limits: "gas" one moment, "brake" the next; once worked through, it becomes iron endurance and stamina.
  • Moon ☍ Venus — a seesaw between the need for comfort and one's desires, often played out through relationships with women.
Don't divide aspects into "good" and "bad". Challenging aspects are the engine of growth: without them a chart is talented but passive. Harmonious aspects are resources you can lean on in difficult times.
